# Consent v0.1

## Goal

Make permission understandable, specific, provable and reversible throughout the Legacy lifecycle.

## Consent dimensions

Every consent decision specifies:

- purpose: preserve, transcribe, retrieve, generate, share, export or research;
- data: text, photo, audio, video, biometric template, relationships or private conversations;
- actor: creator, named person, family group, trustee, processor or researcher;
- modality: original playback, summary, synthetic text, voice, image or video;
- duration and review date;
- life state: active, incapacity, posthumous;
- revocation and deletion consequences.

## Consent Center

The interface must answer in plain language:

- What did I allow?
- Who can access it?
- What can AI do with it?
- Does permission continue after my death?
- How do I revoke it?
- What remains after revocation and why?

## Revocation semantics

Revocation stops future processing and sharing. Derived indexes and caches must be deleted or rebuilt where feasible. The historical receipt remains in a restricted audit log to prove that past processing had a legal basis. Revocation cannot remove another person's independent legal rights, but it can stop Memoria from using the disputed item until resolved.

## Third-party data

A creator cannot grant unlimited rights over another person's private conversation, face, voice or sensitive story. Upload flows must collect source, relationship, ownership and permission status. The product needs takedown, restriction and dispute workflows before family collaboration scales.

## Prohibited patterns

- preselected sensitive consent;
- consent hidden in a general terms document;
- "continue" buttons that imply voice or likeness permission;
- withdrawal that requires contacting support;
- punitive loss of export or deletion rights after revocation.
